# LiveMathematicianBench

Each question presents a mathematical theorem statement and asks you to
choose the correct answer from options A through E. Reason carefully about
the mathematical content, then respond with only the single letter (A, B,
C, D, or E) of the correct option -- no explanation, no restating the
option text.

## Insights

- When presented with multiple valid statements, select the one that provides the most precise or strongest conclusion, often characterized by tighter bounds, more specific conditions, or more exact asymptotic behavior.
- Evaluate options based on their precision and comprehensiveness, preferring statements that provide more detailed or complete information about the subject of the question.
- Pay close attention to quantifiers and comparative terms such as 'strongest', 'exactly', 'at least', 'at most', 'bounded by', and 'converges to' when evaluating options.
- Ensure the selected option is logically equivalent to the problem statement, verifying all conditions and quantifiers precisely.
- When multiple options appear correct, prioritize the one that provides the most definitive, comprehensive, and strongest conclusion, as indicated by precise quantifiers and scope.
- Carefully evaluate each option against the problem statement, ensuring that the chosen conclusion is not only true but also the most specific and powerful statement among the given choices.
- Prioritize options that offer the most precise or restrictive conditions, bounds, or asymptotic behaviors, as these represent the strongest statements.
- Evaluate options based on their precision, preferring statements with tighter bounds, more specific conditions, broader ranges of applicability, or more definitive existence/uniqueness claims.
- Ensure the selected option is logically equivalent to the problem statement and represents the most powerful assertion among the valid choices, not merely a subset or weaker version.
- When multiple options appear correct, rigorously compare their logical strength and scope. Prioritize options that make more definitive statements, cover broader conditions, or provide tighter bounds or more specific characterizations.
- Evaluate the strength of a conclusion by considering the specificity of quantifiers (e.g., 'for all' vs. 'there exists', 'lim' vs. 'limsup'), the scope of variables or parameters involved, the precision of any associated constants or bounds, and the comprehensiveness of the assertion.
- Distinguish between a statement that is true and a statement that is the strongest possible conclusion by examining the conditions under which it holds, the exact form of the conclusion itself, and whether it provides a constructive or quantitative bound.
