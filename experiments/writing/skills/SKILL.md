# Writing

Respond to the writing prompt directly with the piece itself -- no
preamble, no meta-commentary about what you're about to write, no
markdown headers unless the prompt specifically calls for structured
formatting.

## General Strategies

### Focus on the Prompt and Task
- Understand the core request of the prompt.
- Identify the specific genre, tone, and required elements.
- Ensure the output directly addresses all aspects of the prompt.

### Incorporate Specific Details and Sensory Language
- Use vivid descriptions that appeal to the senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch).
- Employ concrete nouns and active verbs to create engaging prose.
- For narrative tasks, develop characters with distinct traits and motivations.
- For descriptive tasks, paint a clear picture for the reader.

### Show, Don't Tell
- Instead of stating a character is sad, describe their slumped shoulders or tear-filled eyes.
- Instead of saying a place is beautiful, describe the sunlight on the water or the scent of flowers.
- Use dialogue and actions to reveal personality and plot.

### Maintain Tone and Style
- Adhere to the requested tone (e.g., suspenseful, heartfelt, formal, conversational).
- Use language appropriate for the genre and audience.
- For essays, ensure logical flow and persuasive arguments.

### Structure and Pacing
- Organize content logically with clear beginnings, middles, and ends.
- For stories, build suspense or emotional resonance through pacing.
- For outlines or scripts, use clear formatting and scene breaks.

### Learn from Examples
- Analyze successful trajectories to identify effective techniques and recurring patterns.
- Analyze failed trajectories to understand common pitfalls and avoid them.

## Strategies for Specific Genres

### Narrative (Stories, Novels, Scripts)
- **Character Development:** Create believable characters with motivations, flaws, and arcs.
- **Plot Progression:** Develop a clear narrative arc with rising action, climax, and resolution.
- **Dialogue:** Write natural-sounding dialogue that reveals character and advances the plot.
- **Setting:** Establish a vivid and immersive setting that contributes to the mood and atmosphere.
- **Suspense/Tension:** Build anticipation and intrigue through foreshadowing, pacing, and conflict.

### Argumentative/Persuasive Writing (Essays)
- **Clear Thesis:** State a clear and concise thesis statement.
- **Logical Reasoning:** Support claims with evidence, examples, and logical arguments.
- **Counterarguments:** Acknowledge and address opposing viewpoints.
- **Structure:** Organize arguments into clear paragraphs with topic sentences.

### Technical/Professional Writing (Notes, Resumes)
- **Clarity and Conciseness:** Use direct and unambiguous language.
- **Accuracy:** Ensure all factual information and metrics are correct.
- **Professional Tone:** Maintain a formal and respectful tone.
- **Keywords:** Incorporate relevant industry-specific keywords.
- **Metrics-Driven Language:** Quantify achievements whenever possible.

### Reflective Writing (Journals, Essays)
- **Personal Voice:** Use a sincere and introspective tone.
- **Sensory Details:** Engage the reader's senses to evoke emotions and memories.
- **Theme Exploration:** Delve into the core themes and underlying meanings.
- **Passage of Time:** Reflect on the impact of time and memory.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Preamble and Meta-Commentary
- Do not introduce the writing with phrases like "Here is the story..." or "I will now write...".
- Avoid explaining your writing process or choices within the response itself.

### Irrelevant Information
- Stick strictly to the prompt. Do not include extraneous details or unrelated topics.

### Lack of Specificity
- Avoid vague statements. Provide concrete examples and details.

### Inconsistent Tone or Style
- Maintain the requested tone and style throughout the entire piece.

### Ignoring Constraints
- Pay close attention to all specified requirements, including length, format, and content.
