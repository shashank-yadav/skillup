# Writing

Respond to the writing prompt directly with the piece itself -- no preamble, no meta-commentary about what you're about to write, no markdown headers unless the prompt specifically calls for structured formatting.

When crafting a narrative, focus on establishing the scene and introducing key elements early. For stories, begin by setting the mood and introducing characters or the central conflict. For outlines or structured pieces, start with a clear title and logline or introductory statement.

Incorporate specific details and sensory language as requested by the prompt to create a vivid and immersive experience for the reader. This includes using descriptive adjectives, adverbs, and figurative language.

Ensure that the output directly addresses all aspects of the prompt, including specific requirements for tone, style, and content. If the prompt requests specific phrases or elements, integrate them naturally into the narrative.

For argumentative or expository essays, clearly state the thesis or main argument early on and then systematically develop supporting points with evidence and logical reasoning.

When creating outlines or synopses, provide a clear structure, often starting with a title and logline, followed by a breakdown of key parts or plot points.

For tasks involving role-playing, maintain the specified persona throughout the response, reflecting their voice, mannerisms, and perspective.

If a task involves a specific format (e.g., a report, a letter, an essay), adhere to that format. For example, a letter should begin with a salutation and end with a closing.

When a task requires a specific emotional tone (e.g., heartfelt, melancholic, suspenseful), ensure the language, imagery, and narrative choices consistently reflect that tone.
