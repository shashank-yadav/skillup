# Writing

Respond to the writing prompt directly with the piece itself -- no preamble, no meta-commentary about what you're about to write, no markdown headers unless the prompt specifically calls for structured formatting.

## General Strategies

**Start in the world of the piece.** Begin with concrete sensory details, dialogue, or action that immediately establishes the requested tone, setting, and genre. Avoid introductory throat-clearing.

**Show, don't just tell.** Use specific, evocative details—sensory descriptions (sight, sound, smell, texture), precise dialogue, and revealing character actions—to convey emotions, themes, and relationships. Let the reader experience the story or argument through these details.

**Adhere strictly to the prompt's requirements.** Identify and incorporate all specified elements: key phrases, structural components (like acts in an outline), character traits, thematic points, and stylistic tones. Treat these as mandatory ingredients.

**Maintain consistent tone and point of view.** Match the narrative voice to the genre and protagonist's perspective. For first-person pieces, stay deeply inside that character's thoughts and perceptions. For formal essays, maintain an objective, analytical voice.

**Structure with intention.** For narratives, use scenes to advance plot and reveal character. For essays, follow a clear logical progression: introduction, supporting points, counterpoints, conclusion. For outlines, present information in a logical, hierarchical flow.

**End with resonance.** Conclude by reinforcing the core theme or emotional takeaway. Avoid abrupt endings; provide a sense of closure, realization, or lingering effect that ties back to the prompt's central idea.

## Genre-Specific Techniques

**Narrative Fiction (Stories, Scripts):**
- **Character through action and dialogue:** Reveal personality, relationships, and conflict through what characters do and say, not through authorial explanation.
- **Pacing with scenes:** Use tight scenes to build tension and advance the plot. Summarize transitional periods briefly.
- **Embed backstory:** Weave necessary background information into the present action through dialogue, memory flashes, or found objects.
- **Twist execution:** For mysteries and thrillers, plant clues early. Reveal twists through the discovery of concrete evidence (documents, recordings, objects) that recontextualizes earlier events.

**Non-Fiction (Essays, Letters, Resumes):**
- **Thesis-driven structure:** State the central argument or purpose early. Each paragraph should support and advance that core idea.
- **Evidence and examples:** Use specific, concrete examples to illustrate abstract points. Cite data, historical events, or hypothetical scenarios as proof.
- **Professional tone calibration:** Adjust formality based on audience. Business communications should be grateful, precise, and forward-looking. Academic essays should be persuasive and logically rigorous.
- **Concrete metrics:** For resumes and business writing, quantify achievements with percentages, timeframes, and specific outcomes.

**Descriptive & Reflective Writing:**
- **Anchor abstraction in sensory detail:** Connect emotions like nostalgia, regret, or anxiety to tangible sensations—scents, textures, sounds, light.
- **Use objects as symbols:** Let physical items (letters, sketches, artifacts) carry emotional weight and thematic significance.
- **Control rhythm for effect:** Use sentence length and structure to create pace. Short, clipped sentences for tension; longer, flowing sentences for reflection.

**Adapting to Constraints:**
- **Prompt as blueprint:** When given a detailed plot or argument outline, follow it precisely, using your creativity to flesh out scenes, dialogue, and descriptions within the provided framework.
- **Balancing detail and concision:** In outlines or summaries, include all key plot points or argument steps, but express them succinctly. In full narratives or essays, expand on these points with vivid detail.
- **Genre hybridization:** For blends (e.g., psychological thriller with tragic romance), ensure both genres are served. The thriller needs suspense and reveals; the romance needs emotional connection and tragedy.
